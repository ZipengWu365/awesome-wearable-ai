## [Self-Supervised Learning of Accelerometer Data Provides New Insights for Sleep and Its Association with Mortality](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01065-0)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Develops accelerometer-based sleep classification and applies derived sleep duration and efficiency measures to mortality analyses in UK Biobank.
- **Why it matters:** Links representation learning, digital sleep measurement and population outcomes in a single reproducible pipeline.
- **Limitations:** Agreement with polysomnography is only fair to moderate and mortality analyses remain observational with a single baseline wear period.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.

## [Deep Learning for Automated Sleep Staging Using Instantaneous Heart Rate](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-0291-x)

- **Year / source:** 2020 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Develops automated sleep staging from instantaneous heart rate, targeting wearable-compatible cardiac sensing rather than full polysomnography.
- **Why it matters:** Shows how a lower-burden physiological signal can support longitudinal sleep-stage estimation outside specialist laboratories.
- **Limitations:** Heart-rate-based staging cannot recover all information in polysomnography and performance may shift across arrhythmias, medications, age and devices.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02
