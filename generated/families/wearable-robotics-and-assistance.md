## [Overground Gait Training With a Wearable Robot in Children With Cerebral Palsy: A Randomized Clinical Trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2821278)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · JAMA Network Open · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `intervention` · `clinical-evaluation` · `randomized-controlled-trial`
- **Contribution:** Randomized clinical trial compared torque-assisted overground robot gait training with matched conventional physical therapy in children with cerebral palsy.
- **Why it matters:** Provides comparative clinical evidence for power-as-needed wearable robotic assistance.
- **Limitations:** Eligibility and device size restricted the studied population; optimal dosing and durability remain uncertain.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported effect is tied to the randomized intervention, comparator, population, follow-up and endpoint; it should not be generalized to other devices or care pathways without new evidence.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Soft Robotic Apparel to Avert Freezing of Gait in Parkinson's Disease](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02731-8)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · Nature Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `intervention` · `closed-loop-deployment` · `clinical-deployment`
- **Contribution:** Sensor-driven soft robotic garment delivered hip-flexion assistance and was evaluated through repeated assisted and unassisted walking in one person with Parkinson's disease.
- **Why it matters:** Illustrates a genuine sensing–decision–actuation wearable loop targeting an acute motor impairment.
- **Limitations:** N-of-1 proof of concept; efficacy, safety, usability and durability across patients are unknown.
- **Evidence boundary:** Repeated within-person effects are compelling mechanistic evidence for this participant but cannot establish population-level effectiveness.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02
