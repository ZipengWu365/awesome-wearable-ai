## [Effect of Wearing a Novel Electronic Wearable Device on Hand Hygiene Compliance Among Health Care Workers: A Stepped-Wedge Cluster Randomized Clinical Trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2776020)

- **Year / source:** 2021 · JAMA Network Open · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `intervention` · `clinical-evaluation` · `randomized-controlled-trial`
- **Contribution:** Stepped-wedge cluster randomized trial tested real-time wearable feedback on hand-rub duration and volume among healthcare workers.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a useful negative example showing that sensor feedback can improve action quality without improving the intended behavioural frequency.
- **Limitations:** Single geriatric hospital, volunteer wearers and strong time/Hawthorne effects complicate transportability.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported effect is tied to the randomized intervention, comparator, population, follow-up and endpoint; it should not be generalized to other devices or care pathways without new evidence.
