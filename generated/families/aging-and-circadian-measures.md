## [From Wrist Data to Lifespan: Elucidating Inflammation-Driven Biological Aging via Activity Rhythms Captured by Wearable Devices](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00349-x)

- **Year / source:** 2026 · npj Aging · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Evaluates wearable-derived activity rhythms in relation to inflammatory biological aging and lifespan-related outcomes.
- **Why it matters:** Connects behavioral rhythm features with a plausible biological pathway and broadens wearable aging measurement beyond chronological-age prediction alone.
- **Limitations:** Pathway analyses and longitudinal associations are vulnerable to residual confounding and do not prove that rhythm modification reverses biological aging.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.

## [Longitudinal Digital Phenotyping of Activity Rhythms and Biological Aging Using Commercial Wearables](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76147-6)

- **Year / source:** 2026 · Nature Communications · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Links multi-year commercial wearable activity rhythms with longitudinal clinical biomarker-derived biological aging trajectories in All of Us participants.
- **Why it matters:** Extends one-week cross-sectional digital aging work toward repeated, person-level trajectories using consumer devices and linked clinical biomarkers.
- **Limitations:** The analysis remains observational, the linked sample is a subset of the cohort, and device-derived rhythm features may depend on wear patterns and proprietary preprocessing.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Circadian Rhythm Analysis Using Wearable-Based Accelerometry as a Digital Biomarker of Aging and Healthspan](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01111-x)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Introduces CosinorAge from seven-day wrist accelerometry and evaluates mortality, incident disease and functional outcomes across UK and US cohorts.
- **Why it matters:** Provides an interpretable example of converting rest–activity rhythm intensity, phase and timing into an externally evaluated digital aging measure.
- **Limitations:** The measurement window was limited, cohorts differed in device placement, and UK Biobank is healthier and less diverse than the general population.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02
