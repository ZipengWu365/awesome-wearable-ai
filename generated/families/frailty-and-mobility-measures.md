## [Daily Activity Patterns from Wearable Accelerometry Predict Physical Frailty and Concern about Falling](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02863-4)

- **Year / source:** 2026 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Uses free-living chest accelerometry and multi-task sequence modeling to jointly estimate frailty and concern about falling.
- **Why it matters:** Illustrates how daily temporal organization and complexity may augment questionnaire-based geriatric assessment.
- **Limitations:** The cohort is modest, measurements span only 48 hours, and reported discrimination requires external and prospective validation before screening use.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.

## [Digital Assessment of Falls Risk, Frailty, and Mobility Impairment Using Wearable Sensors](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-019-0204-z)

- **Year / source:** 2019 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Evaluates sensor-derived mobility features for classifying falls risk, frailty and mobility impairment.
- **Why it matters:** Provides an early integrated digital-measure framework across related geriatric outcomes rather than a single laboratory gait variable.
- **Limitations:** Performance depends on cohort composition and test protocol, and classification evidence is not equivalent to prospective fall prevention or clinical utility.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.
