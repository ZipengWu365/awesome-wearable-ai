## [Ambulatory Atrial Fibrillation Detection and Quantification by Wristworn AI Device Compared to Standard Holter Monitoring](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01555-9)

- **Year / source:** 2025 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `prospective-observational`
- **Contribution:** Compares wrist photoplethysmography and single-lead ECG algorithms with 24-hour Holter monitoring and extends observation for AF burden.
- **Why it matters:** Evaluates both event detection and burden quantification, which is more clinically informative than a binary alert alone.
- **Limitations:** The sample is limited, device-specific and enriched for evaluated patients; broader screening utility and effect on management require additional trials.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.

- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`

## [Large-Scale Assessment of a Smartwatch to Identify Atrial Fibrillation](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901183)

- **Year / source:** 2019 · New England Journal of Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `prospective-observational`
- **Contribution:** Evaluates smartwatch irregular-pulse notifications followed by telemedicine and ambulatory ECG confirmation in 419,297 participants.
- **Why it matters:** Provides population-scale prospective evidence on notification yield and positive predictive value in real-world smartwatch use.
- **Limitations:** Enrollment was self-selected, only notified participants received confirmatory monitoring, and the design did not estimate population sensitivity or outcome benefit.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.

## [Passive Detection of Atrial Fibrillation Using a Commercially Available Smartwatch](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2675364)

- **Year / source:** 2018 · JAMA Cardiology · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Trains and externally tests a deep neural network on commercial smartwatch photoplethysmography for passive atrial-fibrillation detection.
- **Why it matters:** Established an early proof of concept for longitudinal rhythm inference from commodity wearable pulse data.
- **Limitations:** Performance fell in ambulatory self-reported analysis, the external criterion-standard cohort was small, and prospective screening utility was not demonstrated.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.
