## [Prospective Associations of Daily Step Counts and Intensity With Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease Incidence and Mortality and All-Cause Mortality](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2796058)

- **Year / source:** 2022 · JAMA Internal Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Assesses daily step volume and peak cadence against cancer, cardiovascular disease and mortality outcomes in 78,500 UK Biobank participants.
- **Why it matters:** Provides dose-response evidence for an intuitive wearable measure across several public-health endpoints.
- **Limitations:** The cohort is predominantly White, step exposure is based on a limited wear period, and observed associations do not prove benefits of prescribed step targets.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Wearable Accelerometer-Derived Physical Activity and Incident Disease](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-022-00676-9)

- **Year / source:** 2022 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Tests accelerometer-derived moderate-to-vigorous physical activity against 697 incident disease outcomes in 96,244 UK Biobank participants.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates the breadth of phenome-wide association possible with standardized wearable activity measures and linked longitudinal health records.
- **Limitations:** One-week exposure measurement, selection into UK Biobank, multiple testing and residual confounding limit causal interpretation and individual risk use.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Association of Daily Step Count and Step Intensity With Mortality Among US Adults](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2763292)

- **Year / source:** 2020 · JAMA · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Relates accelerometer-measured daily steps and cadence to all-cause, cardiovascular and cancer mortality in a nationally representative US cohort.
- **Why it matters:** Anchors widely used step-count measures to long-term outcomes and separates total volume from intensity.
- **Limitations:** The exposure was measured once for up to seven days and the observational design cannot identify an intervention target or eliminate reverse causation.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02
