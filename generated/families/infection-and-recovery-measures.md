## [Wearable Data Reveals Distinct Characteristics of Individuals with Persistent Symptoms after a SARS-CoV-2 Infection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01456-x)

- **Year / source:** 2025 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Identifies pre- and post-infection behavioral and physiological characteristics associated with persistent fatigue and dyspnea.
- **Why it matters:** Shows why pre-event wearable baselines matter when interpreting post-viral trajectories and potential risk markers.
- **Limitations:** The persistent-symptom subgroup was small and pre-existing differences limit claims that infection caused the observed digital phenotype.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Long-term Changes in Wearable Sensor Data in People with and without Long Covid](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01238-x)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Compares step count, resting heart rate and sleep trajectories for up to one year after infection in participants with and without Long COVID.
- **Why it matters:** Illustrates person-specific recovery curves and persistent deviations that episodic clinic visits can miss.
- **Limitations:** Long COVID was self-reported, groups differed at baseline, and trajectories cannot by themselves identify mechanisms or treatment effects.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Passive Detection of COVID-19 with Wearable Sensors and Explainable Machine Learning Algorithms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-021-00533-1)

- **Year / source:** 2021 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Builds an explainable wearable-based COVID-19 detection model that can operate with missing symptoms and heterogeneous available sensor streams.
- **Why it matters:** Addresses deployment realities such as incomplete self-report and variable consumer devices.
- **Limitations:** Predictive performance declines when restricted to pre-test passive data, and prospective alert utility and false-positive burden require evaluation.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Pre-symptomatic Detection of COVID-19 from Smartwatch Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-020-00640-6)

- **Year / source:** 2020 · Nature Biomedical Engineering · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Uses individualized deviations in heart rate, steps and sleep to identify physiological changes around COVID-19 and other illnesses.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates the value of personal baselines for detecting acute deviations before or near symptom onset.
- **Limitations:** The number of confirmed cases was small, analyses were largely retrospective, and alert specificity across other stressors and infections remains challenging.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.
