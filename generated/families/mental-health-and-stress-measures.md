## [Accurately Predicting Mood Episodes in Mood Disorder Patients Using Wearable Sleep and Circadian Rhythm Features](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01333-z)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Uses personalized sleep-wake and circadian features to predict next-day depressive, manic and hypomanic episodes.
- **Why it matters:** Illustrates individualized longitudinal prediction from low-burden wearable-compatible variables.
- **Limitations:** The cohort is modest, episode labels and missingness may be informative, and prospective decision support or intervention benefit was not tested.
- **Evidence boundary:** This supports analytical or clinical validation in the studied cohort and device setting; it does not establish universal cross-device validity or downstream clinical benefit.

## [The Real-World Association between Digital Markers of Circadian Disruption and Mental Health Risks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01348-6)

- **Year / source:** 2024 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Derives wearable-based circadian misalignment measures and relates them bidirectionally to mood in medical interns undergoing shift-work transition.
- **Why it matters:** Shows how multiple internal rhythms and sleep timing can be combined into interpretable longitudinal mental-health markers.
- **Limitations:** The cohort is occupation-specific and observational; bidirectional associations and time-varying confounding limit causal interpretation.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02

## [Large-Scale Wearable Data Reveal Digital Phenotypes for Daily-Life Stress Detection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-018-0074-9)

- **Year / source:** 2018 · npj Digital Medicine · `sci_journal`
- **Type / stage:** `measure` · `measurement` · `external-validation`
- **Contribution:** Analyzes five days of free-living wearable physiology, context and self-report from 1,002 participants to characterize daily-life stress phenotypes.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates why ambulatory stress models require personalization and population-scale heterogeneity rather than laboratory signals alone.
- **Limitations:** Stress labels are self-reported, the study is cross-sectional, and physiological response may reflect many non-stress processes.
- **Evidence boundary:** The reported relationship is observational or predictive. It does not establish that changing the wearable-derived measure will causally change the clinical outcome.
- **Verification:** `primary_source_checked` on 2026-09-02
