01 · SENSE

Wearable sensors capture body and context signals.

ECG records electrical heart activity; EEG records brain activity; CGM tracks interstitial glucose. Devices measure different signals—not a complete picture of health.

Other inputs include optical pulse (PPG), motion (IMU), muscle activity (EMG) and respiration. Sleep is inferred from signals, not a sensor type.

02 · REPRESENT

Models turn raw signals into features for prediction.

A representation is a set of learned features that summarizes the signal. Other models can use those features to recognize an activity or estimate risk; accuracy must be tested on new people and devices.

03 · THE LIFECYCLE

Ten stages connect sensing to adaptive action.

A useful system must do more than predict: check signal quality, select an action, evaluate its effects and use the observed response to update future decisions.

05 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES

Each research claim requires different evidence.

A link between two measurements is not proof of cause. Accurate forecasts do not tell us whether a treatment will help. Choosing when to act and showing patient benefit require separate tests.

Policy value means the expected outcome of a rule for choosing actions. Clinical utility means benefit when a system is used in care.
