01 · PREDICTIONEmerging pattern

What might happen to this person?

Models forecast body signals and answer questions about health histories

SOTER predicts future signal values and fills missing readings. WearableQA tests questions about months of personal data. These are tools for future health-prediction systems, not clinically validated disease predictors.

SOTER · 15 Sep 2026 / WearableQA · 4 Sep 2026 · Preprints

MethodSOTER adapts general time-series modelling to physiology: it learns relationships between signals, routes different frequency patterns to specialized model components, and predicts values at requested times.
DataWearableQA adds 4,084 questions, 200 people and histories up to 500 days.
Performance51.2% → 71.3% question-answering accuracy (+20.1 percentage points). On WearableQA, the same GPT-5.4 improves when allowed to run Python calculations instead of only reading the data as text. This changes the tools and compute, not the model version.
Model resultsWithout training on the target dataset, SOTER reports the lowest root-mean-square error (RMSE) on 4 of 6 datasets and mean absolute error (MAE) on 5 of 6. Both measure prediction error; lower is better.

What this does not prove These preprint results do not establish better diagnosis or patient outcomes. SOTER also includes hospital signals, not only everyday wearable data.

02 · INTERVENTIONEvidence checkpoint

What changes under a different action?

Causal frameworks and intervention trial results

Causal digital twins propose how to compare treatment alternatives. Earlier controlled trials provide a baseline for judging whether a personalized action improves an outcome.

Causal framework · Feb 2026 / AID trial · May 2025 · Earlier baselines

FrameworkMove from predicting what happens next to estimating what would change under a different treatment, then choosing a sequence of actions. This is a proposed framework, not a tested treatment benefit.
Trial baselineIn a 72-person, six-month automated insulin delivery (AID) trial, adjustments guided by a person's glucose–insulin simulation improved the time spent within the target glucose range. Information feedback alone did not show an additional improvement.
This review weekNo new intervention outcome was verified in the selected weekly window.

What this does not prove Changing a model input is not the same as changing treatment in a trial. A simulated benefit still needs real-world testing.

03 · DIGITAL TWINEmerging pattern

Can we build an evolving model of this person?

Diabetes twins add repeated updates and feedback

A personal digital twin is a model of one person that can be updated with their data. The studies here focus on diabetes, not a simulation of the entire person.

T2D predictive twin · Jul 2026 / AID trial · May 2025 · Earlier baselines

Type 2 diabetesThe July 2026 study adds weekly model retraining and daily text-message suggestions. In a small substudy (10 people receiving AI feedback; 9 controls), mean weight loss was 5.87 vs 3.57 lb over the three-month intervention. Glucose levels remained stable. Study details ↗
Type 1 diabetesThe separate 2025 trial uses individual glucose–insulin simulations to adjust automated insulin delivery and replay alternative actions. It is not the same system as the type 2 diabetes study.

What this does not prove Forecasting glucose, simulating physiology and estimating treatment effects are different capabilities. These studies do not establish a whole-person digital twin.

04 · METAVERSE & MIXED REALITYProduct updates

How can a person see and interact with their twin?

Smart glasses add developer tools; avatar calls are announced

The near-term change is not a complete health twin. It is a more programmable wearable interface combining sensing, input and display.

Meta DAT 1.0 & Display · Sep 2026 · Vendor announcements

Developer toolsMeta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit (DAT 1.0) moves from preview to a supported release. Apps can use glasses cameras, speech, head motion and Neural Band wrist input.
HardwareDisplay glasses and wrist neural input: broader availability is announced, not a new independently tested sensor-performance gain.
Product featuresSelected US retail → more markets and online ordering. Voice-driven face-avatar calls are announced for later early access; delivery depends on region.
Possible useGlasses could let people view information from, or send input to, a personal health model. This is a possible application, not a demonstrated health-twin product.

What this does not prove These are vendor announcements, not independent tests. A face avatar is not a body model; a glasses display is not necessarily content anchored in the physical world.

Quick definitions: digital twins, counterfactuals and evidence labels
Counterfactual
A “what if?” comparison: what might happen under a different action. Changing a model input alone does not prove a real treatment effect.
Digital twin
A model of a particular person or system, updated using observations. Check which signals it models and whether its simulations were tested.
Representation / features
A summary learned from signals that can be used for tasks such as activity recognition or risk prediction.
Policy value
The expected outcome of following a rule for choosing actions over time—not a government policy.
Clinical utility
Whether using a system improves patient or care outcomes, considering harms as well as benefits.
Review depth
How much curators checked: publication details, core metadata, or a study summary with limitations. This is not a score for scientific quality.
Signal abbreviations
ECG: electrical heart activity. PPG: optical pulse. EEG / MEG: electrical / magnetic brain activity. EMG: muscle activity. IMU: motion sensors. CGM: continuous glucose monitoring. PSG: a multi-signal sleep study.
Foundation model
A model pretrained for reuse across tasks. The library includes other model types too; not every model record is a foundation model.

01 · SENSE

Wearable sensors capture body and context signals.

ECG records electrical heart activity; EEG records brain activity; CGM tracks interstitial glucose. Devices measure different signals—not a complete picture of health.

Other inputs include optical pulse (PPG), motion (IMU), muscle activity (EMG) and respiration. Sleep is inferred from signals, not a sensor type.

02 · REPRESENT

Models turn raw signals into features for prediction.

A representation is a set of learned features that summarizes the signal. Other models can use those features to recognize an activity or estimate risk; accuracy must be tested on new people and devices.

  • calibrate
  • encode
  • learn

03 · THE LIFECYCLE

Ten stages connect sensing to adaptive action.

A useful system must do more than predict: check signal quality, select an action, evaluate its effects and use the observed response to update future decisions.

04 · THE ATLAS

The atlas connects six research resource types.

Browse models, datasets, analysis methods, health measures, interventions, and tools or standards. These describe the type of resource; the lifecycle shows where each resource is used.

05 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES

Each research claim requires different evidence.

A link between two measurements is not proof of cause. Accurate forecasts do not tell us whether a treatment will help. Choosing when to act and showing patient benefit require separate tests.

Policy value means the expected outcome of a rule for choosing actions. Clinical utility means benefit when a system is used in care.
