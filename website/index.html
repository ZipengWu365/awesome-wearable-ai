Small signals. Big possibilities.

01 · SENSE

Intelligence begins with small signals.

A wrist pulse. A change in gait. A night of sleep. Wearables turn continuous life into measurements—but every modality sees only part of the person.

Motion, ECG, EEG, PPG, EMG, glucose and sleep each require different assumptions.

02 · REPRESENT

Signals become machine-readable structure.

Models organise noisy streams into representations, patterns and predictions. The transformation is powerful; it is not the same as clinical truth.

  • calibrate
  • encode
  • learn
  • interpret

03 · THE LIFECYCLE

One model is only one moment in a longer loop.

Real-world impact depends on the chain around it: quality, measurement, prediction, causal reasoning, intervention, and learning from what happens next.

04 · THE ATLAS

A field this connected needs a map.

Six resource types form the atlas. The categories describe what a record is; the lifecycle describes where it helps. They are related, but not interchangeable.

05 · EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES

Prediction is not the end of the story.

An association does not guarantee prediction. A prediction does not establish a causal effect. A causal estimate does not automatically define a safe policy, and policy value does not guarantee clinical utility.

The atlas keeps these claim layers visible—especially when the distinctions are inconvenient.

The registry separates evidence cards, verified metadata, and venue-screened records. It tracks whether a source was checked against a primary paper, official resource, or review reference. Coverage is curated—not claimed to be exhaustive.

What might happen to this person?

From signal encoding to longitudinal reasoning

Physiology-aware generation and long personal histories move the field beyond static representations toward richer temporal reasoning.

MethodGeneral transfer → cross-channel structure, frequency-aware expert routing and continuous-time decoding.

Boundary SOTER and WearableQA are preprints; benchmark gains are not diagnosis accuracy or clinical utility.

02 · INTERVENTIONEvidence checkpoint

What changes under a different action?

Action claims still need intervention evidence

The technical update is a sharper separation between counterfactual frameworks, adaptive policies and observed outcomes in controlled trials.

FrameworkPrediction → causal models, potential outcomes and sequential policy optimization.
Trial baselineA 72-person AID study supports bounded twin-guided co-adaptation; feedback alone added no effect.
This review weekNo new intervention outcome was verified in the selected weekly window.

Boundary A model-level what-if, a causal estimate and an intervention result answer different questions.

03 · DIGITAL TWINEmerging pattern

Can we build an evolving model of this person?

Useful twins remain disease-specific

Recent systems combine individual adaptation, repeated updates, feedback and what-if replay—but within tightly bounded physiological problems.

BeforeStatic personalization or periodic self-monitoring without a continuously updated action model.
NowWeekly retraining, daily suggestions and person-specific glucose–insulin simulation.
Observed outcomeIn a 19-person T2D substudy, weight differed while glucose stayed stable.

Boundary Predictive, mechanistic and causal twins are not interchangeable; no whole-person twin is established.

Inspect twin evidence ↗
04 · MIXED REALITYProgrammable entry point

How can a person see and interact with their twin?

Glasses are becoming an integration surface

The near-term change is not a complete health twin. It is a more programmable wearable interface combining sensing, input and display.

Developer stackMeta DAT 1.0 moves from preview to supported tooling for camera, speech, head motion and Neural Band input.
Hardware/productDisplay availability expands and voice-driven face-avatar calls are announced.
New directionGlasses can become an interaction entrance to a personal model rather than the model itself.

Boundary Vendor announcements are not independent tests; an avatar is not a physiological twin and a HUD is not automatically spatial MR.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY NEW?

Six lenses for judging an update

A new paper title is not enough. The project records what changed, compared with what, under which evidence, and what remains unproven.

01Methods

Physiology-aware temporal modeling, causal frameworks and supported glasses APIs.

02Data

Longitudinal reasoning benchmarks now pair multi-month wearable histories with structured questions.

03Hardware

Display glasses and wrist neural input are moving toward broader—but region-dependent—availability.

04Performance

Large gains appear when tool access changes; results must stay tied to the same benchmark and protocol.

05Products & features

Developer preview becomes supported release; hands-free avatar calls enter the product roadmap.

06New directions

Long-history reasoning, bounded personal twins and mixed-reality access begin to converge—without proving a full system.

NEW

Richer longitudinal context, explicit tool use, disease-specific adaptive twins and programmable glasses interfaces.

NOT YET

No prospective clinical utility for the new preprints, no new weekly intervention result, and no validated whole-person twin.
